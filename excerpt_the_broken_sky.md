# Excerpt: The Broken Sky
### From Arc 7: The False Nemesis

The tip of my plum blossom blade was pressed against the Heavenly Demon’s throat.

Blood—thick, black, and smelling of burnt sulfur—dripped from his severed pauldron, hissing as it hit the cracked obsidian floor of the summit. Around us, the storm clouds that had shrouded the continent in eternal twilight for sixty years swirled like a dying whirlpool.

The war was over. 

Down in the valleys, hundreds of thousands of imperial soldiers and sect warriors were cheering my name until their lungs tore. The boy from Southfall Village. The resurrected beggar who rebuilt a dead clan from the mud. The hero who had finally brought down the monster of the age.

I leaned on my sword, my ribs screaming, my breath coming in ragged, bloody wheezes. I looked down at the demon lord pinned beneath my boot, and let out a dry, venomous chuckle.

"Get up, you old bat," I mocked, wiping a smear of red from my split lip with the back of my sleeve. "Is that all you had? You spent sixty years terrorizing the land, burning provinces, making emperors wet their beds, and you fold after thirty exchanges? What a rip-off. I broke my favorite training stick for this?!"

The Heavenly Demon didn’t roar. He didn’t curse.

He just lay there against the broken dais, his white hair plastered to his forehead with sweat, and let out a sound.

It was a laugh.

A quiet, wheezing, agonizingly human laugh.

"Chung Jin," he whispered, his crimson eyes dimming as he looked up at me. "You truly are a disciple of the Northern Heaven. Fast as lightning. Stubborn as a mule. And blind as a newborn pup."

"Flattery won't stop me from taking your head," I scoffed, pressing the iron a fraction of an inch deeper. "Give me one good reason I shouldn't carve you into kindling right now."

"I don't need a reason, boy," the Demon murmured, coughing up a mouthful of black bile. "I'm already dead. You pierced my life-gate five minutes ago."

He raised a trembling, armored hand and tapped his own shattered breastplate.

"I just wanted to live long enough... to watch your face when you look up."

My brow furrowed. "Look up at what?"

With a sickening, hollow crack, the Demon's dantian shattered.

The dark, suffocating miasma that radiated from his flesh—the infamous Demon Shroud that had covered the skies of Earth since the year I woke up in the mud—did not explode. 

It evaporated.

Like morning mist burned away by dawn, the black clouds parted. The thick, sulfurous veil that had hidden the stars for sixty years dissolved into nothingness, pulled apart by the high-altitude winds.

For the first time in two centuries, the true night sky was revealed to the human race.

The cheering in the valley below abruptly stopped.

It didn't fade. It died in an instant, replaced by a silence so absolute, so suffocating, that I could hear the blood dripping from my knuckles onto the stone.

Then, from ten miles below, came the screams.

Millions of voices, crying out in raw, unadulterated, mind-shattering horror.

I slowly looked up.

There were no stars.

The sky was not black.

The Moon—the silver pearl of the night that mothers sang about to their infants—was not a rock. 

It was an open, colossal, unblinking mechanical iris. 

A sphere of polished, geometric chrome spanning thousands of miles across the void, covered in glowing, interlocking rings that shifted and spun with cold, silent mathematical perfection.

And right now, every single ring was focused down on us.

*HUMMMMM.*

A vibration passed through the marrow of my bones. High in the upper atmosphere, ten thousand red lights ignited simultaneously.

Targeting reticles. 

Orbital laser arrays, each one capable of drilling a hole through a mountain, painted the surface of the earth in a web of crimson pinpricks. One by one, every city, every fortress, every imperial academy across the continent was marked.

"You fool," the Heavenly Demon whispered, his cold fingers weakly curling around the flat of my blade to steady his dying breath. "You glorious, righteous, stupid fool."

He smiled—a tragic, bitter smile that broke the last pieces of my heart.

"Who told you I was invading the world? The academies? The emperors? The sector chiefs?" 

A tear cut a clean path through the soot on the old warlord's cheek.

"I wasn't conquering them, Jin. I was keeping the curtain drawn. My miasma was the only electromagnetic shield thick enough to blind their orbital cameras. I spent sixty years playing the monster so those metal bastards in the sky couldn't see where the livestock was sleeping."

His grip on my sword slipped, his arm falling limp onto the stone.

"Now... the clever hero has killed the scarecrow."

His eyes rolled back, his chest falling still.

"And the cattle have nowhere left to hide."

The wind on the peak turned freezing cold.

Far above, in the silent black of space, the lunar iris clicked. A massive, continent-spanning kinetic strike began to charge, illuminating the underside of the clouds with a sickly, ionizing glare.

In the valley below, soldiers dropped their weapons and fell to their knees in the mud, weeping, praying to gods that didn't exist.

I stood alone on the mountain.

My sword was shaking. My knees wanted to buckle. 

*We didn't win,* the voice whispered in the back of my skull. *You did it again. Your sword doomed them all.*

And then, deep in the darkest, most bruised corner of my chest, something snapped.

The Plum Blossom Sword Heart didn't flicker. It erupted. 

A wild, manic, completely unhinged laughter tore out of my throat, ringing across the dead peak.

I kicked the Heavenly Demon's corpse aside, planted my boots on the edge of the precipice, and raised my notched, blood-soaked blade straight toward the great white eye in the sky.

The red targeting beam centered squarely between my eyebrows, burning warm against my skin.

I didn't blink. 

I gave the Moon the widest, most psychotic wolf-grin of my two-hundred-year life.

"You've been peeking through the keyhole for two centuries, have you?!" I roared up into the vacuum, my voice challenging the thunder of the charging weapon. 

"WAKE UP DOWN THERE, YOU COWARDS! PICK UP YOUR STEEL!"

I leveled my blade at the cold chrome god of the heavens, my knuckles white, my soul burning with pure, unrepentant martial fury.

"YOU WANTED A WAR WITH HUMANITY?! THEN DON'T BLINK, YOU OVERGROWN TIN CAN—BECAUSE MOUNT HUA IS COMING TO THE MOON!"
