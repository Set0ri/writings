All of it. A lie. A cattle pen built by gods who lived on the Moon.

I wiped the white coolant from my filleting knife and looked up at the sky.

High above, through a tear in the smoke, the pale crescent moon hung cold and indifferent in the black void.

*They have my coordinates now,* I thought, feeling the chilling certainty settle into my bones. *An orbital strike won't be far behind.*

I turned back to my father.

"Father," I said, helping him up from the bloody clay. "Get Mother out of the cellar. Gather what dry grain you can carry, and take the villagers into the deep caves on the western cliffs. The sky is going to look for me here."

"Jin," Daesung choked out, gripping my arm with his uninjured hand. His eyes were wide with grief, but for the first time, he saw me clearly. Not as a boy who had fallen from a ridge, but as an ancient, unyielding mountain of iron. "Where will you go?"

"To the mainland," I replied, a slow, wild grin breaking across my face in the firelight. 

"To find the ruins of Mount Hua."

"And when I find them... I'm going to teach this world how to pick up a sword again."

***

Dawn broke over the gray expanse of the ocean.

At the edge of the rocky wharf, a small, two-masted fishing cutter bobbed against the swell. 

My mother, Yeon, stood on the wet stone, her hands trembling as she fastened a thick, oiled wool cloak around my shoulders. She didn't ask questions. She didn't weep. A mother knows when her child carries a storm inside his ribs.

"Eat your rice on the crossing, Jin-ah," she whispered, kissing my cheek with lips that tasted of salt tears. "And remember... no matter what sits in the sky, you are my son."

"I know, Mother," I said, offering her a rare, gentle bow.

I stepped onto the cutter's wooden deck and untied the mooring line. 

The sail caught the cold northern wind, billowing taut as the little boat cut through the volcanic surf, leaving Southfall Village and the burning palisade behind.

I stood at the bow, leaning on my bamboo pole, looking west toward the vast, dark silhouette of the continent rising on the horizon.

Deep in my chest, the Plum Blossom Sword Heart beat with steady, thunderous power.

*Two hundred years of silence.*
*The world in chains.*
*The sky watching every move.*

I raised my face to the cold spray of the sea and laughed.

"Hang on, disciples," I muttered toward the mainland. 

"Your ancestor is coming home."
