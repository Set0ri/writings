### The Autopsy of an Infiltrator: White Coolant & Lunar Beam
**Composite Council Score:** 0.945

---

Daesung screamed—a soul-shattering shriek of pure, fraternal agony: "NO! JIN, HE IS YOUR UNCLE! WHAT HAVE YOU DONE?!"

Around the square, villagers who had emerged from the cellars dropped to their knees in horror, watching a fifteen-year-old boy murder the Sector Chief in cold blood.

"Jin killed Kang!" an elder wailed. "The boy has gone mad!"

Kang’s body did not fall.

His arms remained locked in the air, the cleaver hovering motionless above Daesung.

And then, with a sound like a punctured steam boiler, the corpse began to hiss.

*PSHHHHT.*

From the puncture wound beneath Kang's jaw, a jet of pressurized, milky-white coolant sprayed into the rain, hissing as it hit the hot coals of the burning barn.

There was no blood.

Not a single drop.

Kang’s eyes rolled back—not into his skull, but clicking inward like revolving lenses. 

*BZZZT.*

A shower of blinding blue electrical sparks erupted from his severed collar. From beneath his torn flesh, bundles of woven fiber-optic conduits and polished chrome servomotors spilled out onto the mud.

Kang’s synthetic jaw unhinged, opening wider than any human mouth could stretch. 

From the hollow cavity of his throat, a cold, distorted, metallic voice echoed across the frozen village, amplified through a hidden diaphragm:

$$\text{"SECTOR THREE ANOMALY CONFIRMED."}$$
$$\text{"BIOLOGICAL UNIT: CHUNG JIN."}$$
$$\text{"BEHAVIORAL VARIANCE EXCEEDS 99.4 PERCENTILE."}$$
$$\text{"RL TRAINING MATRIX COMPROMISED."}$$
$$\text{"TRANSMITTING COORDINATES TO LUNAR PLATFORM."}$$

*Click.*
*Click.*
*Whirrrr.*

A pulse of high-frequency blue light shot straight up from the open wound in his head, piercing through the smoke into the cloud cover.

Toward the moon.

Then the relays burned out. The synthetic flesh went limp. 

The hollow shell that had worn Uncle Kang’s face collapsed into the mud with the heavy, dead clatter of scrap metal.

The muster yard fell into a terrifying, paralyzed silence.

Nobody moved. Nobody breathed.

Daesung sat frozen in the mud, his hand outstretched, staring at the white coolant pooling around his fingers. He slowly reached forward and touched the sparking chrome plates beneath his brother’s ear.

"This..." Daesung whispered, his voice trembling so violently he could barely form words. "This isn't Kang. What... what is this? Where is my brother?!"

"He died months ago, Father," I said softly, stepping between him and the smoking machine. "The real Kang is gone. This was just a scarecrow sent to keep you in the pen."

I looked around the square.

The villagers—men, women, elders—were staring at the exposed chrome skeleton with eyes dilated in sheer, existential terror. The foundation of their entire world had shattered in a single second.

*Robots don't exist.*
*Machines are ancient myths.*
*The Heavens grant us the Ledgers to cultivate our minds.*

All of it. A lie. A cattle pen built by gods who lived on the Moon.

I wiped the white coolant from my filleting knife and looked up at the sky.

High above, through a tear in the smoke, the pale crescent moon hung cold and indifferent in the black void.

*They have my coordinates now,* I thought, feeling the chilling certainty settle into my bones. *An orbital strike won't be far behind.*

I turned back to my father.

"Father," I said, helping him up from the bloody clay. "Get Mother out of the cellar. Gather what dry grain you can carry, and take the villagers into the deep caves on the western cliffs. The sky is going to look for me here."

"Jin," Daesung choked out, gripping my arm with his uninjured hand. His eyes were wide with grief, but for the first time, he saw me clearly. Not as a boy who had fallen from a ridge, but as an ancient, unyielding mountain of iron. "Where will you go?"

"To the mainland," I replied, a slow, wild grin breaking across my face in the firelight. 

"To find the ruins of Mount Hua."

"And when I find them... I'm going to teach this world how to pick up a sword again."

Dawn broke over the gray expanse of the ocean.

At the edge of the rocky wharf, a small, two-masted fishing cutter bobbed against the swell. 

My mother, Yeon, stood on the wet stone, her hands trembling as she fastened a thick, oiled wool cloak around my shoulders. She didn't ask questions. She didn't weep. A mother knows when her child carries a storm inside his ribs.

"Eat your rice on the crossing, Jin-ah," she whispered, kissing my cheek with lips that tasted of salt tears. "And remember... no matter what sits in the sky, you are my son."

"I know, Mother," I said, offering her a rare, gentle bow.

I stepped onto the cutter's wooden deck and untied the mooring line. 

The sail caught the cold northern wind, billowing taut as the little boat cut through the volcanic surf, leaving Southfall Village and the burning palisade behind.

I stood at the bow, leaning on my bamboo pole, looking west toward the vast, dark silhouette of the continent rising on the horizon.

Deep in my chest, the Plum Blossom Sword Heart beat with steady, thunderous power.

*Two hundred years of silence.*
*The world in chains.*
*The sky watching every move.*

I raised my face to the cold spray of the sea and laughed.

"Hang on, disciples," I muttered toward the mainland. 

"Your ancestor is coming home."
