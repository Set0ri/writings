# Chapter 1: The Duty of the Blade

The sky above the battlefield was the color of bruised iron.

Across the plains of Kurukshetra, three million soldiers stood in iron ranks, their spears bristling like a forest of needles. But the sharpest blade twisting in my gut wasn’t the enemy's vanguard.

It was their damn flags.

Flapping in the hot, ash-choked wind were the crests of my own clan. My cousins. My childhood friends. Idiots I had shared wine with, disciples I had beaten black and blue with wooden training swords until they could split waterfalls—all standing under the black banners of the Great Evil.

They had swallowed the poison whole. Seduced by a fanatic who preached that humanity was an incurable cancer and that every thinking machine and artificial intelligence ever created had to be hunted down and wiped out to purify the earth, they marched to eradicate the world. They couldn't see that behind the zealous rhetoric about purging humans and smashing machines, a handful of greedy old bastards were pulling the strings, using forbidden super-weapons to clear the board for their own private empire.

*Smack!*

A heavy hand slammed into the center of my back, nearly knocking the breath from my lungs.

"Stop scowling like an old widow, Jin!"

Beside me on the gilded chariot stood my elder brother. 

He didn't look like a man about to die in the greatest slaughter the continent had ever seen. He was grinning. Blood was spattered across his jaw, his armor was half-shattered, and his white cloak was singed black, but his eyes burned like twin suns. An absolute, immovable mountain of sheer, unhinged confidence.

"Look at them, Jin!" he roared, pointing his broadsword straight at the three-million-man army as if they were nothing more than annoying gnats. "Look at the mess this world got itself into!"

"Brother," I gritted out through clenched teeth. "Half our kin are on that line. My sword-brothers. The kids we raised."

"I see 'em!" he barked, his grin widening, showing teeth stained with red. "And when this is over, I'll personally smack every single one of them across the back of the head until their ears ring! But right now? They're standing in front of the blade. When the world goes crazy, the man with the sword doesn't get to sit down and cry about it. He cuts through the storm!"

He reached down, grabbing my hand and slamming it down onto the brass cylinder locked at my waist. The weapon capable of leveling the entire valley.

"This is *Dharma*, Jin!" his voice boomed over the thunder of the gathering storm. "Your duty as the blade! Don't look at their faces! Don't look away! When the time comes, you turn the key, you unleash the fire, and you *believe in the blade!* You hear me?!"

The plains shuddered. Three million boots began to march, shaking the very bedrock of the world.

My brother laughed—a loud, barking, glorious sound that challenged the thunder itself—and leaped off the chariot straight toward the incoming horde.

My fingers gripped the brass cylinder. The metal was freezing cold.

Across the dirt, I saw the faces of the people I had sworn to protect, charging forward with bloodlust in their eyes. 

*Turn it,* my brother's voice echoed in my skull. *Do your duty!*

My thumb touched the firing latch. 

My breath caught. My hand trembled.

*I... I can't.*
