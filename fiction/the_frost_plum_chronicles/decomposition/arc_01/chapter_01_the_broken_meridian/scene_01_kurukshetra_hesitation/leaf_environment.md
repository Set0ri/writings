The sky above the battlefield was the color of bruised iron, pressurized with ozone and pulverized hematite that tasted like copper pennies on the tongue. Across the plains of Kurukshetra, three million soldiers stood in unbroken iron ranks, their spears bristling like a forest of black needles against the hot, ash-choked wind. But the sharpest blade twisting in my gut wasn’t the enemy vanguard—it was their damn flags. Flapping in the scorched gale were the crests of my own clan: cousins, childhood friends, disciples I had beaten black and blue with wooden practice swords until they could split waterfalls, now marching under the black banners of the Great Evil.
