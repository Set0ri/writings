Instead, I sat cross-legged on the straw mat, took a slow breath through my teeth, and began cycling the **Gentle Breath of the Morning Dew**.

*Inhale: cold mist, salt, and the faint tang of pine ashes.*
*Circulate: down through the throat, past the cracked collarbone, into the hollow cavern of the lower abdomen.*
*Compress: igniting the tiny, stubborn violet ember resting against my spine.*

Every cycle sent a pulse of liquid warmth radiating through my chest. It wasn't the roaring river of Qi I had wielded at Kurukshetra. It was a drop. A single, stubborn bead of condensed life-force, no larger than a grain of sand.

But a single spark in dry grass is enough to burn a forest.

By the time the third morning bell clanged from the watchtower, the dull ache in my fifth rib had subsided from a stabbing agony to a manageable throb. I washed my face in the rain barrel outside, tied my father’s small iron stone-chisel to the inside of my right forearm with a strip of leather, and headed down the central lane.
