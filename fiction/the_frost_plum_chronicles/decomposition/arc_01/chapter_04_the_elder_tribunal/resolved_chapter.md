### The Council Hall Reckoning
**Composite Score:** 0.952

The Southfall Council Hall was the only tiled structure in the settlement, built from heavy fir timbers with stone hearths at either end. Braziers burned with green pine charcoal, casting flickering shadows across the faces of seven village elders seated on low bamboo mats. Outside, the villagers huddled in the freezing sleet, peering through the open doors with anxious, hollow eyes.

Uncle Kang stood at the center of the hall, clad in an immaculate silk-trimmed coat, holding an open parchment roll.

*"Elders,"* Kang proclaimed, his voice resonant and smooth as oiled brass. *"The security levy for the northern palisade is delinquent by thirty-four silver pieces. My brother Daesung has failed to meet his woodcutting quota for three consecutive cycles. Under the founding charter, his cottage, timber plot, and the ancestral plum orchard must be transferred to the Sector Administration to cover the debt."*

Daesung stood before the council, his head bowed, his calloused hands trembling against his coarse linen trousers.

*"Brother Kang,"* Daesung rasped, his voice breaking. *"That orchard was planted by our grandfather. It's the only land our family has left..."*

*"The charter does not recognize sentiment, Daesung,"* Kang said coldly, without a flicker of emotion. *"Only arithmetic."*

The elders looked down at their laps, muttering among themselves in cowardly silence. None dared oppose the man who controlled the militia and the winter grain rations.

Elder Song, his whiskers yellowed with pipe smoke, raised the wooden gavel to ratify the foreclosure.

*BANG.*

The heavy double doors of the hall flew wide open, banging flat against the timber pillars as a blast of icy wind extinguished three braziers.

### The Entrance of the Dried Anchovy
**Composite Score:** 0.962

---

The silence that fell over the council hall was absolute, broken only by the rhythmic tap-tap-tap of a knotted wooden stick on the polished flagstones. Snowflakes drifted through the doorway, melting upon the heated stone floor like dying moths.

Down the central aisle strolled Jin.

He wore an oversized hemp tunic that flapped around his paper-thin frame like sails on an abandoned skiff. In his left hand, he held a stalk of dried barley, chewing on it with insolent leisure. In his right hand, he balanced his three-foot knotted oak branch, using it as a cane with exaggerated, dramatic decrepitude.

Beside him walked Cousin Bao, scrubbed clean, his head held low, carrying a water-stained leather satchel under his arm.

"Jin?!" Daesung gasped in horror. "What are you doing out of bed?!"

*"Taking a walk, Father,"* Jin said cheerfully, waving the oak stick at the elders like a schoolteacher greeting rowdy toddlers. *"The air in our shack was getting stuffy, and I heard there was an auction of ancestral graves going on."*

Kang's eyes narrowed, his left hand dropping to the hilt of his short sword. *"Insolent brat. Guards, remove this lunatic before he defiles the tribunal."*

Two burly militia guards stepped forward with iron truncheons raised.

Jin didn't even look at them. *"Touch me, and I'll have the Magistrate's Tax Inspector hang you from the palisade by your own belt straps. Bao—throw the book at him."*

### The Great Reversal: 300 Sacks of Grain
**Composite Score:** 0.965

---

The hall grew suffocatingly warm as the villagers outside pushed through the doorway, their murmurs growing into an angry, mutinous roar. The smell of burning tallow mingled with the stench of sudden sweat from Uncle Kang's forehead.

Bao slammed the leather satchel onto the council table.

Out spilled a bundle of water-logged ledgers, bound with hemp twine and stamped with the personal signet of the Black Wave Bandits.

Kang's chief enforcer, a scarred brute named Gwan, lunged forward with a drawn iron dagger to seize the papers.

Jin didn't move his feet. With a lightning flick of his wrist, he redirected the oak branch through a clean three-inch arc, tapping the funny-bone on Gwan's elbow.

*CRACK.*

The enforcer shrieked in agony as his ulnar nerve seized; the dagger flew from his numb fingers, burying itself in the council rafters, while Gwan crashed face-first through a decorative cedar screen.

Jin planted his oak branch on top of the ledgers, leaning forward with his terrifying Mount Hua tiger-grin.

*"Let's do some arithmetic, Uncle,"* Jin sang out, his voice ringing across the rafters. *"Fifteen tons of communal barley smuggled to the Gull's Maw sea caves over three seasons. Four crates of weapon-grade iron traded for private gold ingots. It seems the Sector Administration isn't owed thirty-four silver pieces... the Sector Administration owes this village three hundred sacks of emergency winter grain!"*

The elders erupted in uproar. Kang's jaw clicked violently, his eyes flashing with microscopic bursts of static violet light as his internal processors calculated the legal collapse.

*"Sign the release, Uncle,"* Jin whispered, leaning close enough to smell the faint, metallic scent of cold coolant behind Kang's ears. *"Cancel the debt, deed the orchard to my father, and deliver three hundred sacks by sunset tomorrow... or I burn these ledgers right on the Magistrate's desk."*

Kang's fingers trembled, his pen scratching across the parchment with frantic, mechanical desperation.
