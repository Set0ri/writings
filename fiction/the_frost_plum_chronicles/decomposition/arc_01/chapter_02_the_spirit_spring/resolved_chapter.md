Past the perimeter wattle fence of Southfall, the mountain sloped down into a ravine choked with frozen blackberry thorns and silver-barked birch trees. A trickle of clear spring water seeped from between two granite boulders, steaming faintly in the pre-dawn frost. The air tasted of pine needles, wet moss, and bitter mountain minerals.

Jin dragged his aching body through the wet brambles, his bare toes turning purple against the ice-crusted loam. He knelt by the spring and plunged his face straight into the freezing mud, drinking deeply of the raw mineral runoff.

Jin opened his eyes, wiping the bloody mucus from his lip with a vicious smirk. *"One thread. That's all this ancestor needs to start breaking bones."*

### The Father's Filial Chains
**Composite Score:** 0.953

---

The dawn light revealed the squalor of Daesung's woodyard: piles of damp cordwood, a dull iron splitting maul, and a sagging chicken coop containing two half-plucked hens. The morning chimney smoke from neighboring houses was thick with the scent of fried salt-fish, while Daesung's hearth produced only thin, bitter ash.

Daesung was on his knees by the splitting log, coughing into a rag stained with flecks of dark blood. 

*"Father,"* Jin said quietly, stepping through the wattle gate.

Daesung quickly tucked the bloody rag into his belt, forcing a strained, exhausted smile onto his weathered face. *"Jin-ah... you shouldn't be out in the cold. Your fever only broke yesterday."*

*"Why do you take it?"* Jin demanded, leaning on his oak branch. *"You have the broad shoulders of a veteran hunter. Kang is your younger brother, yet he treats you worse than a stray mule. Throw him into the manure ditch and be done with it."*

Daesung's eyes turned dim and hollow. *"You don't understand, son. On her deathbed, your grandmother took my hands. She made me swear by the ancestral tablets: 'Daesung, Kang was born frail. Protect him. Endure him. Never raise a hand against your blood.' A man without filial honor is no better than an animal, Jin."*

Jin stared at his father's bowed neck. A knot of hot, bitter rage tightened in his throat—not at Daesung, but at the parasitic cruelty of the world that used a good man's decency as a leash to choke him.

Jin said nothing. He simply walked over to the chopping block, gripped the heavy splitting maul with his scrawny hands, and swung it with clean, effortless skeletal alignment.

*THWACK.*

The knotted pine log split clean down the grain, popping apart like a dry persimmon. Daesung blinked in astonishment as Jin tossed the maul aside without breathing hard.

*"Rest, Father,"* Jin muttered. *"Starting today, this family stops eating dirt."*
