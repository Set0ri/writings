### IX. Southfall Rebirth: The Dried Anchovy & The Oak Stick

The small courtyard of the ancestral Southfall cottage was a squalid portrait of rural neglect: late winter slush, rotting wattle fences, and chickens pecking hopelessly at frozen cabbage stalks. 

The air was bitterly cold, smelling of melting snow, damp firewood smoke, and cold barley gruel. From the eaves, the rhythmic drip-drip-drip of snow-melt struck the icy stones, counting down the seconds until the world realized who had returned.

In the center of the slush, surrounded by rolling abacus beads and cabbage leaves, stood the dried anchovy boy with an oak branch in his hands, his wild hair whipping in the mountain wind, his terrifying wolf-grin defying the five thousand years of silence.

Five thousand years later.

Drip. *Drip.* Drip.

The sound of snow-melt leaking through rotting thatch struck the wooden floorboards with maddening, monotonous precision.

Jin opened his eyes.

He did not see the vast, starry cislunar void. He did not smell cosmic ozone or warm titanium feather-scales. He smelled damp earth, moldering straw, and the sour stench of spilled barley porridge.

He tried to sit up.

*Crrrk.*

A sharp, pathetic spasm seized his lower spine. His breath wheezed in his throat like a broken bellows. He looked down at his trembling hands.

They were not the calloused, iron-forged palms of the Peerless Sword Saint. They were tiny, translucent, skin-and-bone claws. His wrists were the thickness of dry reeds; his ribs protruded against his filthy hemp tunic like the frame of an abandoned kite.

*"A dried anchovy..."* Jin wheezed in horror, his jaw trembling. *"You dog-shit heavens... you ungrateful, rotten celestial bastards! I burned my soul to ash, turned the two keys, and saved your miserable universe... and you reincarnated me into a dried, starving, salted anchovy?!"*

Outside the flimsy paper door, a greasy, nasal voice cut through his outrage:

*"Yeon-ah, you must be reasonable. Your useless cousin has been in a coma for seven days. He won’t last the night! If we sell the ancestral plum orchard to the Merchant Guild, at least you won't starve as a concubine in the magistrate’s manor..."*

Jin’s feral wolf-eyes snapped wide.

A terrifying, unhinged grin—teeth bared, eyes gleaming with murderous mirth—spread across the emaciated face of the boy.

*"Filial piety?"* Jin whispered, rolling out of the bed onto the dirt floor with a muffled thud. *"Family harmony? Oh, Uncle Kang... this ancestor is about to give you a lesson in Mount Hua lineage discipline!"*

Jin crawled toward the hearth on hands and knees, cursing the heavens with every shallow breath.

Beside the cold stone hearth lay a seasoned winter oak branch, roughly three feet long, knotted and gnarled, used for stoking firewood. 

Jin’s bony fingers closed around the rough bark. 

The balance was abominable. The wood was front-heavy and rough enough to give him splinters. But as his calloused memory settled into the grain, the trembling in his limbs stopped. He rose to his feet, testing the branch with a sharp, two-handed swish through the chilly air:

*WHOOSH.*

A sharp cramp shot through his right rotator cuff. Jin winced, spat onto the dirt, and laughed like a hyena.

*"Good enough to beat an unfilial dog."*

With one barefoot stomp, Jin kicked the flimsy paper door straight off its rusted leather hinges.

*BAM!*

The paper door blew outward into the snowy courtyard. Yeon—a scrawny, wide-eyed girl clutching an empty wicker basket—let out a terrified squeak and fainted dead away, tumbling backward into a pile of winter cabbages.

Uncle Kang, fat and clad in greasy rabbit-fur, jumped three feet in the air, dropping his abacus into the snow.
