### The Audience of Empty Bowls
**Composite Council Score:** 0.938

---

The Grand Hall of Mount Hua was fifty paces wide, built of mountain cedar that had once withstood the celestial wars—now reduced to a cavern of shadows and shame. Cold autumn rainwater leaked through fractured terracotta roof tiles, dripping with maddening, funerary cadence into chipped earthenware bowls lined along the flagstones: *plink... plonk... plink.* The air was heavy with the smell of damp mold, bitter chicory roots, and old, suffocating defeat. On the high northern wall, the magnificent silk banner of the Plum Blossom Sect was faded to grey, its golden embroidery long since picked clean and sold for rice. A single iron dish lamp sputtered with cheap, foul-smelling rendered tallow, barely illuminating the two old men huddled by a brazier of ash.

Sect Leader Hyun Jong looked up, his hollow, gentle eyes carrying the weight of a dying lineage. "Child... if you have come seeking martial arts or shelter, we can offer you only boiled roots. Mount Hua has nothing left. Our ancestral debt to the Shaanxi Merchant Guild has come due."

Beside him, Elder Hwang flicked a trembling finger across a wooden abacus missing three of its ivory beads. "Forty-two thousand... forty-two thousand silver taels. By the winter thaw, they will foreclose on Lotus Peak itself."

"FORTY-TWO THOUSAND?!"

Jin's furious roar shook the rotting rafters, dislodging a shower of cedar dust right into Hyun Jong's teacup. Elder Hwang gave a terrified wheeze, clutching his left breast as the abacus clattered across the stones.

"Are you senile old fossils completely out of your minds?!" Jin yelled, his eyes wide and wild with deranged fury as he brandished his oak branch. "How in the name of the Heavenly Ancestor do you owe forty-two thousand taels on a mountain made of bare rock?! Did you hire celestial courtesans to fan you while you slept?! Did you eat roasted phoenix for breakfast?! Did you hand Southern Edge our gold in silk ribbons?!"

Hyun Jong sat frozen, blinking in sheer, paralyzed astonishment. "Young... young man, how dare you speak so irreverently in the ancestral—"

"Shut up!" Jin snapped, stomping toward them. "I'm looking at your books! Hwang! Pick up that abacus before I use it to comb your beard!"

Jin snatched the wooden abacus off the floor with the notched tip of his stick, flicking it upward into his palm with seamless martial leverage. His thumb blurred across the remaining wooden beads, recalculating compound interest figures in a flurry of sharp wooden clacks. He dropped onto one knee, bowing his head to the cold flagstones before the ancestral altar—a gesture of respect that concealed the feral clenching of his jaw and the hot tears stinging his eyelids. Then he slapped the abacus back onto the low table, grabbed a horsehair ink brush, and slammed three savage black strokes onto the disciple ledger.

"Chung Jin," he snarled, throwing the brush onto the desk where it splattered black ink across Elder Hwang's sleeve. "Third-generation disciple. Sign me in right now. Give me a uniform, give me a bowl of porridge, and don't touch that damn front gate until I tell you to. From this moment on, anyone who wants a copper coin from Mount Hua will have to negotiate with my stick!"
