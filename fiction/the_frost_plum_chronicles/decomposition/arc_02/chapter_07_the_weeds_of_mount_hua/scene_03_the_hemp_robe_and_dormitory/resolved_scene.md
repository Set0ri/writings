### The Hemp Robe & The Third-Class Dormitory
**Composite Council Score:** 0.935

---

Night settled upon Mount Hua, biting and needle-sharp. The third-class dormitory was little more than a drafty wooden lodge where cross-gales whistled through torn paper lattice screens, dropping the room temperature down to near freezing. Ten thin straw pallets lined the bare cedar floor, where nine teenage orphans huddled beneath coarse, lumpy quilts, shivering in their sleep with teeth clicking like dry castanets. The iron woodstove sat hollow and cold, holding only ancient birch ash, while pale blue moonlight spilled across the floorboards in cold, indifferent stripes.

Yoon Jong, the earnest third-class dormitory leader, handed Jin a bundled hemp garment with two hands, his expression a mixture of duty and exhaustion. "This is your uniform, Chung Jin. Treat it with care. Morning practice begins at the fifth watch sharp. Do not be late."

Jin unfolded the robe, held it up to the moonlight, and scowled in profound disgust. "What kind of horsehair sandpaper is this?! Are you trying to scour my hide off before breakfast?! It smells like wet turnips!"

"Keep your voice down!" Yoon Jong hissed, frantically glancing toward the paper partition. "The first-class seniors are sleeping in the west wing! If Senior Brother Baek Cheon hears you making a commotion—"

"Baek Cheon? What kind of ridiculous name is that? Sounds like an overcooked rice bun," Jin grunted, tossing the robe over his shoulder. "Tell that bun to sleep soundly while he still has teeth. Tomorrow, this mountain is getting an education."

Jin pulled the coarse hemp robe over his thin shoulders. The rough weave scratched against his collarbone like river gravel, but as his fingers brushed the faded plum crest embroidered over his chest, his breath hitched in his throat. Five thousand years. A cosmic war, a dying brother's roar, a solar lotus detonating in the black void... and now, the cold night air of Mount Hua filled his lungs once more. He sat on the rough straw pallet, tucking the knotted winter oak branch beneath his arm like an old friend's sword. Closing his eyes, he initiated the quiet, steady rhythm of the Morning Dew breath, circulating trace Qi through his calcified meridians. "Just wait, you old bastards," he whispered into the frozen dark, his lips curling into a feral, protective smile. "Your grandfather is back. And this mountain will never bend its knee again."
