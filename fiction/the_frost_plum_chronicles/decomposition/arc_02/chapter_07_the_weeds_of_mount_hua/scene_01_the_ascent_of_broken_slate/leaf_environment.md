The mountain path was a wound that five thousand years had failed to heal.

Granite stairs, once polished like mutton-fat jade by the boots of ten thousand righteous disciples, climbed into the swirling autumn clouds like a shattered spine at a punishing thirty-eight-degree incline. Cold wind swept down from Lotus Blossom Peak, tasting of high-altitude frost, wild dried berries, and the bitter silence of dead men. Where carved stone dragons had once arched over the mountain gateway, only blackened cedar stumps remained, smothered in wild briars whose crimson thorns clutched at the howling gale. At the first terrace, a massive bronze ceremonial bell hung lopsided from a rotting gallows, its clapper torn away, its surface coated in thick green verdigris like stagnant pond scum. Not a single plum petal drifted on the breeze. Only dead leaves, jagged rocks, and the hollow howl of the empty peaks.
