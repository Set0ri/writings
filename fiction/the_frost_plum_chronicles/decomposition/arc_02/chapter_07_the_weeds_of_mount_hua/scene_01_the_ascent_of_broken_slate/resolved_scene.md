### The Ascent of Broken Slate
**Composite Council Score:** 0.941

---

The mountain path was a wound that five thousand years had failed to heal.

Granite stairs, once polished like mutton-fat jade by the boots of ten thousand righteous disciples, climbed into the swirling autumn clouds like a shattered spine at a punishing thirty-eight-degree incline. Cold wind swept down from Lotus Blossom Peak, tasting of high-altitude frost, wild dried berries, and the bitter silence of dead men. Where carved stone dragons had once arched over the mountain gateway, only blackened cedar stumps remained, smothered in wild briars whose crimson thorns clutched at the howling gale. At the first terrace, a massive bronze ceremonial bell hung lopsided from a rotting gallows, its clapper torn away, its surface coated in thick green verdigris like stagnant pond scum. Not a single plum petal drifted on the breeze. Only dead leaves, jagged rocks, and the hollow howl of the empty peaks.

Jin planted his knotted winter oak branch into the shattered gravel, using it as a crude lever to haul his ninety-pound frame up the nine-hundredth stair. Every step was an iron toll extracted from kindling; his knees made noises like dry firewood snapping under an overloaded cart, and his left eye twitched so violently his eyelid fluttered like a moth in a draft. Clogged meridians along his lumbar spine throbbed with hot needle pricks under the exertion, threatening micro-tears if he dared push his circulation past a crawl. Pacing his ragged breath to a five-count rhythm to spare his malnourished lungs, he dragged himself upward, knuckles white against the oak bark, coughing dry mountain dust.

At the crumbling gatehouse, two shivering teenage boys in patched, threadbare grey robes fumbled with their chipped iron scabbards, their footwork as unsteady as newborn fawns. Their uniforms were bleached nearly colorless from endless washings, the embroidered plum blossoms faded to ghostly grey smudges.

"H-halt!" the taller boy stammered, raising a pitted practice sword with trembling wrists. "Mount Hua is... we are not accepting guests! Go back down the mountain!"

Jin stopped. He stared at the two starving boys. His left eyebrow twitched. Then, with agonizing slowness, he reached out and poked the taller boy's shin with the tip of his knotted oak stick.

*Thwack.*

"Ow! What was that for?!"

"Look at your grip!" Jin roared, his reedy voice echoing off the canyon walls with the fury of a thousand frustrated ancestors. "Are you holding a sword or are you trying to tickle a dying trout?! Your center of gravity is higher than a mountain pheasant! If an enemy blew on your foreheads you'd tumble all the way down to Shaanxi! Where is your Sect Leader? Where are the elders?! Go tell them their ancestor woke up and he's going to break every broom on this mountain over their heads!"
