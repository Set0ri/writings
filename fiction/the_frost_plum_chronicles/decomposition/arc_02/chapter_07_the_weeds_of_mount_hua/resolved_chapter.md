Not a single plum petal drifted on the breeze. Only dead leaves, jagged rocks, and the hollow howl of the empty peaks.

"H-halt!" the taller boy stammered, raising a pitted practice sword with trembling wrists. "Mount Hua is... we are not accepting guests! Go back down the mountain!"

Jin stopped. He stared at the two starving boys. His left eyebrow twitched. Then, with agonizing slowness, he reached out and poked the taller boy's shin with the tip of his knotted oak stick.

On the high northern wall, the magnificent silk banner of the Plum Blossom Sect was faded to grey, its golden embroidery long since picked clean and sold for rice. A single iron dish lamp sputtered with cheap, foul-smelling rendered tallow, barely illuminating the two old men huddled by a brazier of ash.

Sect Leader Hyun Jong looked up, his hollow, gentle eyes carrying the weight of a dying lineage. "Child... if you have come seeking martial arts or shelter, we can offer you only boiled roots. Mount Hua has nothing left. Our ancestral debt to the Shaanxi Merchant Guild has come due."

Beside him, Elder Hwang flicked a trembling finger across a wooden abacus missing three of its ivory beads. "Forty-two thousand... forty-two thousand silver taels. By the winter thaw, they will foreclose on Lotus Peak itself."

"FORTY-TWO THOUSAND?!"

Jin's furious roar shook the rotting rafters, dislodging a shower of cedar dust right into Hyun Jong's teacup. Elder Hwang gave a terrified wheeze, clutching his left breast as the abacus clattered across the stones.

"Are you senile old fossils completely out of your minds?!" Jin yelled, his eyes wide and wild with deranged fury as he brandished his oak branch. "How in the name of the Heavenly Ancestor do you owe forty-two thousand taels on a mountain made of bare rock?! Did you hire celestial courtesans to fan you while you slept?! "Chung Jin," he snarled, throwing the brush onto the desk where it splattered black ink across Elder Hwang's sleeve. "Third-generation disciple. Sign me in right now. Give me a uniform, give me a bowl of porridge, and don't touch that damn front gate until I tell you to.
