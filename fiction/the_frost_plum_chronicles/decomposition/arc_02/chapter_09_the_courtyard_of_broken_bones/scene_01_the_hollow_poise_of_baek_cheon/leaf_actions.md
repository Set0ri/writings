Baek Cheon, the prized prodigy of the first-class disciples, drifted through the sword forms with the effortless poise of a court aristocrat. Every posture was geometrically immaculate for a painting, but biologically hollow: his elbow was cocked fifteen degrees too high, his weight was pitched onto his decorative toes rather than his rooted dantian, and the entire sequence produced barely enough kinetic force to slice a ripe persimmon.
