### The Subterranean Vault of the First Ancestor
**Composite Council Score:** 0.947

---

The subterranean crypt opened into a subterranean cavern forty feet beneath the library's foundation, hewn directly from Mount Hua's pristine white quartz bedrock. The air was cool, ancient, and utterly untainted by mold or rot, smelling of deep mineral seams and untouched mountain groundwater. At the center of the chamber, nine colossal slabs of translucent mutton-fat jade were mounted upon pillars of polished black basalt. Under the darkness of five millennia, the jade breathed with a faint, pulsing green phosphorescence, illuminating thousands of microscopic sword katas carved into the stone with the tip of an immortal's blade.

Jin stepped across the chamber floor with absolute certainty, his straw sandals depressing four concealed quartz pressure plates in the sacred ancestral sequence: *North, Seven, Three, Center*. Deep within the mountain, ancient counterweights swung smoothly on frictionless bronze gears, sealing the entrance behind them with a muffled thud. He dragged a bewildered Yoon Jong by the collar, halting before the central jade monument. Reaching out, Jin placed his palm against the polished stone, closing his eyes as his senses traced the deep, razor-sharp grooves carved five thousand years ago by the Sword Saint of Mount Hua.

"Is this... is this our true sect?" Yoon Jong whispered, his knees buckling as he collapsed onto the quartz floor, tears spilling freely down his soot-stained cheeks as the luminescent green light bathed his face. "All these centuries... we thought our heritage was lost... we thought we were failures..."

Jin stood beside him, looking up at the colossal jade stele where the Twenty-Four Movements of the Plum Blossom Sword danced in frozen stone. For a moment, his feral smirk softened into something ancient, fierce, and infinitely tender.

"Mount Hua never failed, Yoon Jong," Jin said, his voice ringing through the subterranean silence with the iron resonance of an eternal vow. "A flower does not die because winter comes. It rests beneath the snow. Now stop weeping like a widow, pull out your rubbing paper, and start tracing every single stroke before I smack you with my branch. Tomorrow morning, we teach Baek Cheon and the brats how to make petals fall from the sky."
