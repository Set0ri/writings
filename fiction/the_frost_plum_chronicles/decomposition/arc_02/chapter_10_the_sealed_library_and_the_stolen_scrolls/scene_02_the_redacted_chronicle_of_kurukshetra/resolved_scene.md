### The Redacted Chronicle of Kurukshetra
**Composite Council Score:** 0.95

---

The air in the lowest alcove of the pavilion dropped to an unnatural, suffocating chill. It was not the biting frost of Shaanxi's peaks, but an icy, synthetic void—the lingering electrostatic resonance of ancient orbital data redaction. Delicate, feathery crystals of black frost crystallized across the paper lattice screens. The dust motes in the air froze in place, suspended in eerie stasis, as if the room itself held its breath before an impending cataclysm.

Jin stared down at the massive imperial parchment, his wolf-eyes fixed upon the thick black vermilion characters stamped with the Imperial Censor's seal:

*"In the Final Calamity at Kurukshetra, the swordsmen of Mount Hua abandoned their post on the northern ridge, fleeing before the Heavenly Demon and causing the collapse of the imperial vanguard. By divine edict, their lineage is stripped of noble standing for ten thousand generations."*

"The imperial histories wrote this," Yoon Jong stammered, teeth chattering from the supernatural cold radiating off Jin's body. "Every academy teaches it. That is why no noble family sends their sons to Mount Hua... that is why the world despises us..."

Jin didn't scream. He didn't curse. 

He simply stared at the yellowed page. In his mind, he saw Kamina's bloody, smiling face, Shiva's Trishula severing the Demon King's arm in a geyser of synthetic coolant, and the blinding zero-decibel detonation of the White Lotus as his brother turned the key with his dying breath: *"Don't look back, Jin!"*

"He burned his soul to ash to save their rotting world," Jin whispered, his voice so quiet, smooth, and bottomless that Yoon Jong felt his chest tighten in primitive dread. "And the cowards wrote that he ran."

Jin rested his palm upon the heavy stone pedestal supporting the chronicle. Without a shout, without an overt flare of Qi, the microscopic vibration of non-computable martial intent passed through his skin. 

*Crackle...*

The three-hundred-pound granite lectern dissolved from within, collapsing into a heap of fine, powdery white sand beneath the imperial book. The imperial decree slid into the dust, its lead rivets shearing like soft wax. Jin gently closed the cover with two fingers, dusted the grit from his sleeve, and turned toward the darkest corner of the pavilion. "Stay behind me, Yoon Jong," he said, his lips curling back into that terrifying, razor-sharp wolf-smile. "It's time to open our true inheritance."
