### The Merchant Usury Incursion
**Composite Council Score:** 0.94

---

The morning tasted of rust, cold grease, and bitter rain. Sluggish sheets of sleet washed thick red Shaanxi mud across the white granite paving of Mount Hua's front courtyard, churned into deep eight-inch ruts by the iron-shod wheels of three heavy debt wagons. Twenty swaggering enforcers in boiled leather armor leaned against the ancient carved balustrades, chewing dried betel nut and spitting red bile onto the memorial plaques. At the center of the mud stood Guild Master Kang of the Huashan Merchant Syndicate—a man with three chins, five heavy gold rings clinking on his fat fingers, and an oily ledger bound in pigskin clutched beneath his arm like an executioner's warrant.

The lead enforcer, an iron-shouldered brawler with a scar splitting his left ear, slammed his eight-sided iron mace onto the pavilion's lowest stone step. The ancient granite fractured with a sharp, sickening *CRACK*, sending sharp stone splinters skittering into the mud. He lashed out with a heavy boot, catching third-class disciple Jo Gul in the chest and sending the boy skidding ten feet through the freezing slush. Hyun Jong rushed down the veranda stairs with hem soaked in grey water, his trembling hands outstretched in pleading agony: "Guild Master Kang, please! Restrain your men! The interest was paid last month!"

"The ancestral burial grounds?" Hyun Jong's voice broke into a cracked whisper, brittle as dry winter grass. "You would dig up our founders' bones for silver? Have you no shame? Have you no fear of heaven?"

Guild Master Kang laughed—a wet, phlegmy rattle that shook his three greasy chins. He spat red betel juice directly onto the hem of Hyun Jong's faded robe. "Heaven doesn't lend silver at ten percent monthly compound interest, Sect Leader. I do. If you don't sign over the deed to the southern peak before the midday bell, my boys won't just take the land—we'll break every disciple's sword arm so they can beg properly in the streets of Huayin!"

In the shadowed veranda, hidden behind a carved cedar pillar, Jin stood quietly, a half-eaten wild persimmon in his hand. His left eye twitched. His lips slowly peeled back from his teeth in a wide, terrifying wolf-grin.
