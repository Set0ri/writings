### Non-Computable Sword Mechanics
**Composite Council Score:** 0.947

---

Midnight fell upon Lotus Blossom Peak in robes of dark velvet, crystalline silence, and razor-sharp frost. A single iron brazier sputtered with glowing white oak charcoal in the center of the upper terrace, casting flickering copper halos against the towering quartz precipices. The Shaanxi sky above was an infinite dome of glacial diamond dust, so vast and clear that the jagged silhouette of the mountain seemed suspended in the void. Not a whisper of wind stirred the gnarled pine branches; the only sound was the crackle of dry embers and the white, steaming breath of five shivering disciples gathered around their junior.

Jin knelt by the brazier, took a slender pine twig, and carved a complex, non-Euclidean spiral into the frozen soil. 

"The Southern Edge Sect—and every deterministic sword style under heaven—believes the shortest distance between two points is a straight line," Jin said, tapping the twig against the center of the circle. "They maximize velocity, calculate impact mass, and execute algorithmic thrusts. Against human swordsmen, it is deadly. But against an intelligence that can calculate forty billion vectors a second, a straight thrust is already dead before it leaves the scabbard."

He stood, raising the knotted winter branch. With a slight flick of his wrists, the tip began to oscillate at micro-frequencies—not cutting linearly, but drawing an infinitely unfolding petal curve through the brazier smoke. "The Plum Blossom Sword is biological emergence. It has no single trajectory. It is ten thousand petals carried by an unpredictable gust. You do not calculate the strike; you plant the seed, and let the storm choose where it blooms."

Baek Cheon stared at the spiral in the dirt, his mind reeling as months of rigid aristocratic conditioning dissolved in a single revelation. "If the tip oscillates without fixed trajectory... then the recoil torque cannot be dissipated into the ground. It has to be absorbed entirely by the dantian and radial tendons."

Jin gave an unholy cackle, smacking Baek Cheon's shoulder with the oak stick. "Look at that! The pretty boy actually has a brain between his ears! Why do you think I made you carry fifty-pound boulders up Lotus Peak until you threw up your dinner?! Did you think I was just torturing you for fun?!"

"...Weren't you?" Jo Gul muttered from the snow, rubbing his bruised buttocks.

"Well, ninety percent for fun, ten percent for training!" Jin shot back without shame. "Your forearms are finally dense enough to absorb the shock without snapping your wrist bones! Now stop blubbering!"

Beside them, Yu Iseol drew her plain steel sword. Her unblinking dark eyes reflected the red coals of the brazier. Without a word, she lowered her center of gravity, took a half-step forward, and let her wrist flutter. The blade carved a quiet, trembling curve through the frost, releasing a sound like silk tearing in the dark. Jin watched her, his feral grin softening into quiet awe. "...Look at that. The girl is a monster."
