It began with an impossible fragrance.

In the dead of autumn, upon a barren granite precipice where only thorny briars and frozen lichen clung to the rocks, the air suddenly filled with the sweet, intoxicating scent of wild plum blossoms. It was not the sharp pine resin of Shaanxi, nor the bitter coal smoke of the brazier, but the delicate, heartbreaking aroma of crimson petals blooming through thick mountain snow—a sacred fragrance that had not touched the Central Plains in two centuries. The night breeze died away in reverent silence, and even the drifting frost particles seemed to hover in the air, waiting.
