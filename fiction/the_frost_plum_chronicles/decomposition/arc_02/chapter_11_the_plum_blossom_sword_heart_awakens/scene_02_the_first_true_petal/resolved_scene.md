### The First True Petal
**Composite Council Score:** 0.95

---

It began with an impossible fragrance.

In the dead of autumn, upon a barren granite precipice where only thorny briars and frozen lichen clung to the rocks, the air suddenly filled with the sweet, intoxicating scent of wild plum blossoms. It was not the sharp pine resin of Shaanxi, nor the bitter coal smoke of the brazier, but the delicate, heartbreaking aroma of crimson petals blooming through thick mountain snow—a sacred fragrance that had not touched the Central Plains in two centuries. The night breeze died away in reverent silence, and even the drifting frost particles seemed to hover in the air, waiting.

Yu Iseol took a single step forward, her movements as smooth as water gliding over frozen river ice. She abandoned every calculated posture, emptying her mind of fear, debt, and generational despair. Her wrist initiated the high-frequency oscillation Jin had traced in the dirt.

*SHING.*

The slender blade sang—a single, bell-pure acoustic resonance that pierced the midnight stillness. The tip of her sword did not cut in a line; it danced. In the cold starlight, a radiant, translucent pink mirage bloomed at the point of her blade—a pristine five-petaled plum blossom forged of non-computable Qi and pure human devotion. A drifting dead cedar leaf fell into the vortex; in a whisper of parted air, it was severed into five immaculate, symmetrical petals that scattered across the snow.

Silence reigned on the terrace—a silence so profound that the tears trickling down Yoon Jong's jaw sounded like raindrops on stone.

"The plum blossoms..." Yoon Jong choked, collapsing to his knees, his trembling fingers reaching out to touch the five severed leaf fragments. "They were not poetry... they were not legends... the Plum Blossom Sword truly exists..."

Yu Iseol lowered her sword with slow, reverent care. Her porcelain cheeks were flushed, her unblinking dark eyes fixed on the empty air where the pink petal had vanished. "...It bloomed," she whispered, her voice cracking for the first time since Jin had met her.

Jin stepped forward. He did not yell. He did not swing his stick. He reached out and gently ruffled her untamed, wind-tousled black hair with a rough, calloused hand. 

"Of course it bloomed, you crazy brat," he said, his voice husky with five thousand years of buried pride. "You're a disciple of Mount Hua. It has always lived in your blood."

Then he spun around, his wolf-grin returning like a flash of summer lightning as he leveled his branch at Baek Cheon. "Now! Dong-ryong! Your junior sister just blossomed a flower! If you don't bloom one before sunrise, I will shave off your magnificent aristocratic eyebrows and paint plum blossoms on your forehead! Draw your sword!"
