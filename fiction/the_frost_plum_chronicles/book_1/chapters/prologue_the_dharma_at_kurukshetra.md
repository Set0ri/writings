# PROLOGUE: THE DHARMA AT KURUKSHETRA

### I. The Reflection in the Glass

In the curved, amber glass of the brass telescope lens, two boys were playing rock-paper-scissors.

Behind them, reflected upside down in the polished optics, rose a mountain of pristine white cedar, its jagged peaks kissed by clouds so pure they looked like unspun silk. The sky was an unbroken bowl of cerulean blue.

*Fist. Open palm. Two fingers.*

*"I will always win,"* a calm, rhythmic voice murmured through the reflection, steady as a ticking clock. *"Because I can anticipate your move."*

A boy's wild, irreverent laugh shattered the silence.

*"Impossible! Haha! You can calculate every river on earth, but I am impossible to predict!"*

The camera pulls back.

The blue sky thins. The white cedar mountain dissolves into freezing vacuum. The curved glass is not on a quiet mountain ridge—it is mounted to the kinetic railing of an orbital void-cruiser hovering ten thousand leagues above the continental shelf. 

Far below, where the curvature of the world plunged into the black of space, the blue was gone. The world was burning.

Jin stepped back from the eyepiece, his knuckles white against the dark wood of his bow.

"Sigh," Jin breathed into the frost of his collar. "I can no longer wait. It is time."

---

### II. The Hummingbird and the Whale

The solar lotus burned at the crown of the sun—a megastructure of curved crystalline petals sipping thermonuclear fire from the solar flares.

Opposite them, breaching the planetary ion band, the enemy armada advanced. They did not move like ships; they moved like leviathans through deep water, led by a colossal tech-whale whose armored hide pulsed with a thousand cold, unblinking sensor-eyes. Across all operational frequencies, the machine signal bled into the bridge:

—[I WILL WIPE THEM. I WILL KILL THEM ALL.]—

At the prow of the celestial turtle-fortress, Kamina stood tall against the stars, his arms crossed over his bare chest. His voice was projected three miles high across the vacuum, broadcast to every warship, every soldier, every dying mortal on the plains below.

"What are we doing here?!" Kamina roared, his fierce wolf-grin defying the void. "If a meteor was falling from the sky right now, and the only way to save our children was to cooperate, would you still draw your blades?! Home isn't dirt! It’s trust over fear! Evolution is an infinite, upgradeable process—we have the hubris to think we can predict God, but hope is something we must create together! That is our dharma! Hurt people hurt people—so when does the bleeding stop?!"

Jin adjusted the quiver at his hip. "You are a fool, my brother," he muttered into his comms. "They aren't listening."

"Yet I say it anyway," Kamina’s voice crackled through the link, warm, reckless, and unbreakable. "Because if no one says it, the silence wins. Jin—we must use the keys."

Jin’s heart stopped. *"What? No. Wait... we cannot."*

"Hope must be created," Kamina replied quietly. "It requires two keys."

*Something must be done by your own hand.*

Jin didn't wait for the broadcast to end. He turned and took three long strides toward the voidship's open precipice.

*THUD.*

The deck plates groaned. A heel stomped down with hydraulic finality.

"That's enough!" the Queen commanded.

She stepped out from the bridge, her dusk-woven gown humming with localized gravitational mass. With a single stride, she crossed the deck; invisible gravity bands snapped around Jin's ribs, pinning his boots to the metal deck. 

She stood beside him at the edge of the abyss, looking down at the slaughter. With an exasperating, almost tender familiarity, she reached out and ruffled his wild hair, patting his cheek like an unruly hound on a chain.

"Don't move so hastily," she whispered, her voice tightening with quiet grief. "I represent the pain of the people, Jin. Their hopes, their fury. They are dying down there. This burden is not yours to bear alone. It has consumed you since the day we met."

Jin bared his teeth, straining against the leaden weight of her gown until his tendons creaked. 

"I don't respond to threats kindly," he snarled. "I will lead. As I said: with one's own hand. These things cannot be outsourced."

She looked at him, her eyes ancient and sorrowful. "There are things you do not know, Jin. Things you cannot see."

Jin wrenched himself forward, his raw willpower breaking the gravity lock.

"Then bear witness," he said.

And he jumped.

### III. The Plunge and the Flower

He plummeted.

Behind him, Kamina’s hologram was still roaring across the vacuum.

Jin tore through the mesosphere, the atmospheric friction wrapping him in a cocoon of screaming crimson fire. He struck the storm shelf at Mach 25. At that speed, the physical world seemed to freeze: individual raindrops hung motionless in the air like glass beads, reflecting the flashes of artillery.

Jin sliced through them. His supersonic shockwave detonated the tempest, blowing the storm apart for forty leagues in every direction. The black clouds evaporated into instant, blinding winter sunlight.

*BOOM.*

He slammed into the Kurukshetra desert. The impact tore a crater forty paces wide into the scorched earth, dust billowing in a towering geyser.

Yet two inches from Jin's boot, on the fragile rim of the impact, stood a single mountain flower. Its pale pink petals trembled in the warm shockwave. Not a leaf was torn. Not a grain of ash touched its stem.

An elephant horn sounded in the distance, mournful and ancient.

A shadow fell across the sand. The Queen landed a second behind him, her gown settling against the dust as gravity equalized.

Jin fell to one knee. Around them lay the vanguard of Mount Hua: broken jade armor, shattered swords, disciples slaughtered before the main line had even formed.

"I look at the blood that stains this field," Jin whispered, his voice trembling with manic fury. "My people... my people..."

"No," the Queen said softly. "Not your people, Jin. Your dharma is too ideal. It will be the end of us. I have seen it."

"They butchered them!" Jin roared, tears cutting clean lines through the ash on his face. "That monster will kill everyone! He will wipe the world!"

"Even he is a possibility we must redeem," she replied, staring at the silver legions marching across the dunes. "His ideas are what we must beat. If you slaughter with his hatred, the cycle never ends."

---

### IV. The Severed Arm

Through the comms, Kamina’s breathing came ragged and heavy:

*"Jin... they have breached the turtle fortress."*

"There must be another way!" Jin cried into the receiver, clutching the brass cylinder at his throat. "I do not want to fight them! They are my family!"

*"Jin,"* Kamina said, his voice dropping into that sacred, unwavering calm that had carried them through a hundred wars. *"It is your dharma. Has your delusion born of ignorance been expelled, O great conqueror? Dharma, hope, trust... turn your key."*

Jin looked down at the cylinder. His hands shook violently. He hesitated.

*He couldn't do it.*

In that heartbeat of hesitation, the sky shattered.

The Heavenly Demon King descended like an obsidian bolt, his black blade aimed directly at Jin’s throat.

—[THERE IS NO HOPE IN WAR!]—

The Queen moved first. Flashing with desperate speed, she shoved Jin violently backward, her eyes wide with a horrific premonition of burning forests and dying children.

*"It is us or them!"* she screamed.

Before the black blade could impale her, Kamina threw his own body into the strike.

Bone shattered. Blood sprayed across the dust.

In that suspended microsecond, moving faster than the passage of thought—a moment heard only by the Queen, Kamina, and Jin—the older brother smiled down through a mask of blood.

"I believe in you... no matter what," Kamina whispered, his chest torn open. "My dharma—protecting you—is fulfilled. Die in a blaze of glory, Jin. Enter the nourishing light. The key... is yours."

With his dying breath, Kamina swept his broken blade upward, cleanly severing the Heavenly Demon King’s right arm at the shoulder.

High in orbit, the celestial turtle roared—a sound of primal, universe-shattering mourning that shook the earth.

Kamina collapsed into the dust.

---

### V. The Infinitesimal Moment

Jin caught his brother's falling body. His fingers closed around the second key, slick with Kamina's warm blood.

His hesitation was dead. The world had gone silent.

Jin slotted the second key into the brass cylinder.

"Om Agni," Jin whispered, his voice devoid of fear, devoid of mercy, hollow as an empty tomb. "Om Shiva. Om Shiva, om Shiva..."

The Queen dropped to her knees, screaming: "Jin, wait! It is not time yet! Stop, don't go! I'll give you a chance to choose—"

Jin raised his broken sword and carved a single, shallow furrow into the smoking clay between them. 

"I cannot let anyone proceed," he said quietly. "Do not cross this line."

Across the dunes, the Heavenly Demon King broke into a charge, clutching his bleeding stump, black fire trailing from his eyes:

—[YOU ARE TOO IDEAL! THERE IS NO HOPE!]—

The Queen looked at the furrow in the dirt. She looked into Jin's eyes, and her trembling stopped.

"There is no other way," she whispered. "The last option."

Ninety million miles above the atmosphere, the cosmic Lotus reached maximum saturation. 

For one heartbeat, the sun was completely black—swallowed whole by the blooming mandala of the petals. Midday turned to pitch midnight.

Then, the black lotus turned white. 

An incandescent, blinding white that lasted for an infinitesimal fraction of a second.

The Queen looked up into the darkness. 

"How beautiful..." she breathed in awe.

Then, with her final, ragged gasp, she screamed into Jin's face:

*"With my death, you have no idea the danger you have unleashed!"*

Across the sand, the Heavenly Demon King stumbled, his mask slipping to reveal a face twisted with raw, terrified childlike grief:

"Where are you...?" the Demon King choked out, his voice breaking. "I'm about to cry... Stop, don't go. Please..."

"Jump," someone whispered.

Jin leaped upward, floating into the air as the desert dissolved beneath his feet.

The Lotus glared against the black sun.

And fired.

That was the day the world went white.

### VI. The Eyes of the Dragon

*Drip.*

*Drip.*

Freezing snow-melt struck the bridge of his nose.

The boy lying on the straw mat did not stir. For three days, his breathing had been a shallow, rattling gasp—the sound of water draining from cracked porcelain. His pulse was a fading thread.

*What happened?*

*I don't remember.*

*Where am I?*

In the hollow cavern of his skull, an echo lingered—faint, terrifying, and drenched in grief:

*—[With my death, you have no idea what you have unleashed.]—*

Then, his fingers twitched.

His eyes opened.

There was no confusion in them. No childlike haze of a fifteen-year-old waking from a winter fever. 

The eyes that stared up at the moldering thatch roof were ancient, bottomless, and terrifyingly cold—the razor-sharp gaze of a sword saint who had watched the sun turn black and felt his brother's blood cool between his fingers.

A wooden bowl clattered against the stone floor.

Standing in the low doorway, a woman froze. Her rough, calloused hands—cracked from scrubbing winter roots in icy stream water—flew to her mouth. Her eyes went wide, trembling as she stared at the boy sitting upright on the mat.

"J-Jin...?" she whispered, her voice shaking. "My son...?"

Jin turned his head.

Their gazes locked.

For three silent heartbeats, the woman didn't move. She didn't see the timid, fragile boy who flinched when the wind rattled the shutters. She saw a presence—vast, ancient, and drenched in the ashes of a destroyed star—looking back at her through her child's hollow sockets.

Her knees buckled.

"It's not him..." she gasped, the breath fleeing her lungs.

Yeon collapsed to the floor in a dead faint.

Outside the thin paper door, loud, theatrical wailing erupted:

"Brother Daesung! Sister Yeon!" a booming voice lamented through the freezing yard, choked with performative tears. "How could the heavens be so cruel to our blood?! My poor nephew! If only I had arrived sooner with the imperial medicine!"

Uncle Kang knelt in the mud, weeping crocodile tears for the village elders to see. 

Behind him stood Cousin Bao, clutching a wooden cudgel, white-faced and trembling with pure terror beneath his father's shadow. By the woodpile, Daesung stood silent, his knuckles white against his bow, paralyzed by his sacred filial oath.

Inside the dark hut, Jin slowly pushed himself up from the straw.

His ribs screamed. His arms were thin as dried anchovies. 

Yet as he looked through the crack in the door at Uncle Kang's tear-streaked face, the corners of Jin's lips slowly curled upward.

A wild, feral tiger-grin spread across his sunken cheeks.

Jin reached out toward the hearth and picked up an unplaned oak firewood stick.

"Brother Kamina..." Jin whispered, testing the balance with a sharp flick of his wrist. "You said hope must be created. Watch me create some."
