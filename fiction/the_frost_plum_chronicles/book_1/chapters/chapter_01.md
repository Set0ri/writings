### The Cosmic Echo & The Thatch Ceiling
**Composite Score:** 0.956

---

Drip. *Drip.* Drip.

The sound of snow-melt leaking through rotting thatch struck the wooden floorboards with maddening, monotonous precision.

The air inside the room was bitterly damp, thick with the stench of moldering straw, wet ashes, and the sour reek of cold barley porridge. Outside, through the cracks in the paper-screen window, the grey winter sky of Southfall hung low over the sea, heavy with the promise of more sleet. The distant cry of sea gulls cut through the fog like rusty shears.

Jin opened his eyes.

He did not see the starry cislunar abyss. He did not see the gleaming titanium plumage of the *Hummingbird*, nor the blinding solar corona of the Solar Lotus. The image of Kamina's dying wolf-grin and the blinding flash of the White Lotus was still seared onto the back of his retinas, but when he reached out, his fingers touched only coarse hemp and freezing mud.

*"A dried anchovy..."* Jin rasped, his voice a reedy, cracked squeak. *"Five thousand years... I burned my soul to ash, turned the dual keys, scoured the skies of the machine whale... and the dog-shit heavens put me in the body of a starving, salted anchovy?!"*

He stared at his hands in unadulterated fury. The wrists were no thicker than dried winter reeds. The skin was paper-thin and translucent, revealing blue veins that twitched with zero internal Qi.

Jin attempted to throw his legs over the edge of the straw pallet.

*CRACK.*

A sharp, agonizing spasm tore through his lower spine, sending a jolt of fire into his hip. His lungs hitched; his breath whistled in his throat like a clogged flute. His liver glycogen was depleted, his pectoral muscles atrophied down to brittle strings, and his dantian felt like a cold, empty well lined with calcified stone.

He collapsed back into the straw, cursing every deity in the cosmos through gritted, chattering teeth. He couldn't even perform a basic seated circulation without risking a tendon rupture. Pure, unadulterated biological bankruptcy.

### Cousin Bao & The Feral Tiger-Grin
**Composite Score:** 0.954

---

A sudden gust of sea wind rattled the loose clapboards of the shack. Cold slush splattered against the thin door. In the corner of the room, an iron kettle sat cold upon a hearth of soot-stained river rocks, surrounded by a few withered cabbage leaves and a chipped earthenware bowl.

*KABAM.*

The wooden door was kicked off its leather stop, banging violently against the clay wall.

Muddy slush sprayed across the threshold as sixteen-year-old Cousin Bao swaggered inside, flanked by two village youths holding rusted pig-iron spears. Bao wore a greased rabbit-skin vest that barely contained his doughy gut, tracking thick red clay across the floorboards. In the corner, Yeon—a scrawny, hollow-cheeked girl of fourteen wearing a patched hemp coat—shrank back with a frightened gasp.

"Well, look at that," Bao sneered, kicking a wooden stool aside with a loud clatter. "The dead boy finally woke up. Auntie Yeon, tell Uncle Daesung to stop crying over this cripple. My father says if your family doesn't deliver the four baskets of salted fish by sundown, the village council takes the cottage."

Jin slowly turned his head upon the pillow. His half-lidded wolf-eyes locked onto the collar of Bao's vest. 

There, half-hidden beneath the greasy fur, was a dark, purple bruise—the distinct, crushing imprint of four adult fingers, spaced with terrifying, unnatural symmetry.

Jin's lips peeled back into a slow, savage, unhinged wolf-grin.

*"You know, little cousin,"* Jin whispered, his voice dripping with venomous amusement, *"you bark awfully loud for a pig that's already been measured by the butcher's iron tongs."*

Bao choked on his spit, taking an involuntary step back into the slush, his hand flying to his throat in sudden, superstitious terror.

### The Knotted Winter Oak Branch & The Cabbage Faint
**Composite Score:** 0.967

---

Outside in the courtyard, the late winter drizzle fell upon rows of rotting wattle fences and half-buried cabbage pits. A heavy, greasy voice echoed across the slush:

*"Yeon-ah! Be sensible! The boy has been comatose for seven days! He won't survive the thaw! If you sign over the deed to the ancestral orchard, I can still arrange a comfortable concubine position in the magistrate's manor!"*

It was Uncle Kang, standing in his heavy wool coat, clicking the polished wooden beads of an abacus with rhythmic, chilling precision.

*"Filial piety?"* Jin whispered into the dark room, his eyes blazing like twin coals. *"Family care? Oh, Uncle Kang... this ancestor is about to give you a lesson in Mount Hua lineage discipline!"*

He dragged his emaciated frame toward the hearth on hands and knees, ignoring the searing pain in his ribs. Beside the cold stones lay a three-foot length of seasoned winter oak, knotted and gnarled, used for stoking the fire. Jin's bony fingers closed around the rough bark.

The balance was terrible, but the wood was dense, iron-hard, and heavy enough to crack a boar's skull. He gave it two practice swishes through the air: *WHOOSH. WHOOSH.*

*"Good enough to beat an unfilial dog."*

With a sudden surge of skeletal leverage, Jin kicked the flimsy paper door straight off its rusted hinges!

*BAM!*

The door exploded outward into the snowy courtyard. Yeon, carrying an armful of firewood, let out a squeak like a startled mouse and fainted dead away, tumbling backward into a soft pile of winter cabbages.

Uncle Kang jumped two feet in the air, his abacus flying from his grip and scattering fifty wooden beads across the brown slush. 

Before Kang could recover his dignity, the dried anchovy boy stood before him in bare feet and ragged trousers, balancing the knotted oak branch lightly on his shoulder, his terrifying tiger-grin parting the freezing mountain mist.
