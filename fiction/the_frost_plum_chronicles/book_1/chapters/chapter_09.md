### The Hollow Poise of Baek Cheon
**Composite Council Score:** 0.946

---

Dawn broke over Lotus Blossom Peak in shades of bruised amethyst and biting frost. Thick white mountain mist curled across the fractured quartz flagstones of the upper sparring yard, smelling of wet cedar boughs, cold granite, and cheap pine incense. The temperature hovered barely above freezing, pearling heavy dewdrops onto the weapons racks. In the center of the courtyard, twelve first-class disciples stood in two pristine lines, dressed in spotless white-and-black robes with silk lapels that flapped in the brisk canyon wind. Their slender steel swords sliced the morning fog with high, decorative swishes that sounded pretty and threatened absolutely no one.

Baek Cheon, the prized prodigy of the first-class disciples, drifted through the sword forms with the effortless poise of a court aristocrat. His silk sleeves billowed like swan wings; his slender sword cut five graceful petal arcs in the dewy air, concluding with a dramatic spin on one toe and a solemn gaze toward the rising sun. Every posture was geometrically immaculate for a painting, but biologically hollow: his elbow was cocked fifteen degrees too high, his weight was pitched onto his decorative toes rather than his rooted dantian, and the entire sequence produced barely enough kinetic force to slice a ripe persimmon. Behind him, perched atop the rotting wooden fence with his sandals dangling, Jin chewed on a bitter grass stalk, his left eye twitching with homicidal irritation.

*"PFFFFFFFFFT!"*

The sound was so loud, wet, and utterly disrespectful that Baek Cheon's blade froze mid-flourish. The entire courtyard turned in paralyzed shock toward the fence rail, where Jin had just spat his chewed grass stalk into a puddle.

"What on earth was that?" Jin yelled, hopping off the rail and slapping his knees in deranged laughter. "Are you performing at a noble lady's birthday banquet?! Look at those hips! Waving that sword around like a pregnant duck flapping its wings in an autumn bog! Hey! Pretty-boy Dong-ryong! Did you spend three hours in front of the bronze mirror this morning just to swing like a sick maiden?!"

Baek Cheon's face flushed from porcelain pale to violent crimson, a thick blue vein throbbing wildly against his temple. "You... you insolent third-class beggar! How dare you utter that ridiculous nickname in the sacred courtyard?! What right does a fresh novice have to evaluate the sword of Mount Hua's vanguard?!"

"The sword of Mount Hua?" Jin's wolf-smile vanished, replaced by an expression of such cold, lethal contempt that the temperature on the peak seemed to plunge ten degrees. "That toy in your hand isn't Mount Hua's sword. It's a chicken feather. And I'm about to pluck you clean."

### The Wooden Stick Baptism
**Composite Council Score:** 0.959

---

The morning mist was instantly shredded into violent ribbons as the courtyard exploded into acoustic violence. The sharp ringing of steel meeting seasoned winter oak echoed off the granite walls like hammer blows in an imperial armory—*CLANG-THWACK-CRACK!* Disciples went tumbling through the air like dry peas tossed into a hot iron pan, splashing face-first into icy mud and scattering shattered practice weapons across the cracked pavers. The white mountain frost was pulverized into mist beneath the manic, dancing shuffle of Jin's frayed straw sandals.

Baek Cheon launched forward with a furious, blinding thrust—the textbook perfection of Mount Hua's Falling Petal Stance. 

Jin didn't even draw a full breath. Shifting his torso three inches to the left, he let the gleaming tip graze the empty air over his shoulder. The knotted winter oak branch whipped down with lightning bio-mechanical torque.

*CRACK!*

"Your wrist is loose!"

The stick pulverized Baek Cheon's radial bone, knocking the steel blade spinning ten feet into the mist. Before Baek Cheon could comprehend the loss, Jin pivoted on his left ball, his branch tracing a tight circle that cracked Baek Cheon across the top of his skull with the sound of a ripe melon splitting open.

*THWACK!*

"Your head is too high!"

A goose egg the size of a pigeon's egg erupted instantly on Baek Cheon's forehead as he went reeling into the dirt. Second-class disciples Jo Gul and Yoon Jong rushed forward with raised weapons—only for Jin to slide beneath their guard like a greased otter. He hooked Jo Gul's ankle with the curved butt of the stick, sending the muscular brawler flying head-first into an empty rainwater barrel with a hollow *BONG!*, then tapped Yoon Jong on the bridge of his nose so sharply the dormitory leader sneezed three times and sat down hard in the gravel.

"Is this all?!"

Jin stood in the center of the courtyard, the knotted oak branch resting casually across his scrawny collarbone, glaring down at twelve groaning disciples clutching their heads, ribs, and bruised shins.

"Twelve grown men! Twelve sword geniuses of the great Mount Hua! Beaten into porridge in thirty seconds by a fifteen-year-old dried anchovy using a dead tree branch! If the ancestors saw this pathetic display, they wouldn't just weep—they'd claw their way out of the dirt and beat you unconscious with their own femurs! Get up! GET UP, YOU SLUGS!"

Baek Cheon struggled to his knees, his noble aristocratic vanity shattered into dust, tears of furious humiliation mixing with the dirt on his cheeks. "You... what kind of demonic art is that...?"

"Demonic art?" Jin snarled, stepping on Baek Cheon's sword blade and snapping the steel clean in half with his heel. "This is Mount Hua's basic balance, you blind idiot! While you were perfuming your robes, Southern Edge was stealing your rice! From today on, anyone who wants to hold a steel sword on this mountain will have to earn it in blood!"

From the edge of the pavilion, second-class disciple Yu Iseol stepped silently into the light. Her pale face showed no anger—only an unblinking, obsessive hunger. She stared at Jin's wooden stick, then bowed low. "...Teach me." 

Jin's eye twitched as he stared at the silent girl. "...Crazy brat. Fine. You get the biggest rock."

### The Lotus Peak Boulder Run
**Composite Council Score:** 0.956

---

Sunset ignited the jagged crags of Mount Hua into fangs of burning copper and blood-crimson. Below the precipice, the autumn plains of Shaanxi stretched into freezing violet shadows, while the gale howling up the canyon whipped sweat from the disciples' brows and froze it into brittle rime against their temples. The three thousand granite stairs climbed into the burning clouds like an executioner's road, stained with drops of sweat, torn shoe leather, and the sour bile of exhausted boys pushed past every human limit.

Baek Cheon hauled a fifty-pound granite boulder lashed to his bruised spine with rough hemp cord, each step extracting an agonizing shudder from his trembling quads. The coarse rope bit through his silk tunic, rupturing capillaries and smearing red streaks across the white rock. Behind him, Jo Gul collapsed face-first onto stair fifteen hundred, clutching his shins and sobbing dry tears: "I can't... my lungs are bleeding... just leave me to die..."

*THWACK.*

Jin's oak stick struck Jo Gul's backside with explosive ferocity. "Die? Who gave you permission to die on Mount Hua's stairs?! If you die without my permission, I'll go to the Yellow Springs and drag you back by your ear! Get up, you bowl of cold noodles! Look at Yu Iseol! She's carrying a sixty-pound block and she hasn't even blinked! Crawl!"

"Why... why are we doing this?" Baek Cheon wheezed through cracked, bleeding lips as he reached the two-thousandth stair, his vision tunneling into black dots. "What does carrying rocks have to do with the Plum Blossom Sword...?"

Jin, who had been hopping lightly alongside them carrying his winter branch, slowed his pace. For a fleeting second, the unhinged gremlin vanished, replaced by an ancient swordsman whose gaze carried the weight of five thousand years of fallen comrades.

"The Plum Blossom Sword blooms in the dead of winter," Jin said, his voice quiet, slicing through the freezing gale with sudden, absolute clarity. "It doesn't bloom in summer warmth. It blooms when the frost is thickest and every other branch has withered into dust. Southern Edge stole your manuals, took your silver, and convinced you that you were beggars. But they couldn't steal the stone beneath your feet. Today, you carry this mountain. Tomorrow, this mountain carries you."

Baek Cheon stared at the emaciated boy in stunned, tearful silence. His knees locked. With a ragged, guttural roar that echoed across the Shaanxi canyon, he forced his trembling legs forward, hauling the granite boulder into the fading light.
